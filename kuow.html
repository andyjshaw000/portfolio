KUOW Redesign

KUOW, the Pacific Northwest's public radio station, had an aging audience and falling app engagement. They sponsored our capstone to keep current listeners and reach a younger, more diverse crowd.

ContextUW HCDE capstone, sponsored by KUOW
When6 months, 2023
My partResearch, design, video prototype
MethodsInterviews, personas, IA, A/B testing
At a glance2 min read
Problem

KUOW's audience was aging and app engagement was falling.

What I did

Lit review, 8 interviews, 3 personas, IA, Figma redesign, A/B tests with 6 people. I made the video prototype.

Result

A full app redesign and social strategy, pitched to the sponsor and judges.

Planning

We set up sponsor check-ins and a project plan that worked a lot like a PRD, with two tracks: redesign the app, and reach people outside it.

Research

A previous student team found people were confused by the app's organization and wanted cleaner screens and personalized topics. We built on that instead of starting over.

From 8 research papers: many young people expect news to find them on social media, they still care about credible journalism, and location-aware apps could bring them back to local news.

KUOW actually rated better than other NPR station apps, but it had no offline listening or personalized recommendations.

Interviews and usability tests

We ran 8 sessions with people 18 to 29 and over 30: an interview about news habits, then a usability test on the current app.

  • People pull news from everywhere, mixing news outlets with social media.
  • Personal relevance matters. Recommendations from friends and family made sources feel more credible.
  • They wanted plain language and different formats like audio and transcripts.
  • Notification preferences split. Some wanted constant updates, others wanted a set routine.
  • Trust is complicated. It came down to fact-checking, citations, and no clickbait.

In the current app, people struggled with small text, cramped margins, banners that looked like ads, a confusing Mixtape feature, and no dark mode or accessible reader.

Personas, IA, and flows

We built three personas around the behaviors that mattered most: seeking news versus letting it find you, gathering from many sources versus a few, and how each person decides what to trust.

Our three personas

Wireframes

Paper first, then Figma. KUOW's design system arrived late, so we swapped in their colors and icons at the end.

  • Login: profiles, picking interests, and following creators.
  • News: search, sorting, polls, quizzes, and a scroll-to-top button.
  • Live radio: a persistent "Now playing" bar and shareable clips.
  • Podcasts: search, filters, and offline downloads.
  • Profile: notification, interest, and accessibility settings, including dark mode.

Beyond the app

To reach younger people where they already are, we looked at Instagram and TikTok. KUOW's Instagram felt dated, so we proposed a refresh and a more consistent posting schedule.

Testing

We ran usability and A/B tests with 6 participants, comparing the old app with ours and testing the social posts.

A/B tests of the old and new designs

What we changed based on testing:

  • Onboarding felt easy but dull, so we added color, cut white space, made touch targets bigger, and added live password checks and social sign-in.
  • The news feed got card layouts, images, listen buttons, and tappable author names.
  • Live radio got a more obvious listen button, which two participants asked for.
  • Podcasts got more varied artwork, new episodes at the top, and a "continue listening" row. We cut a "From KUOW" section three people found confusing.
  • Social posts got less text, stronger headlines, more visuals, and small topic tags.

Showcase

We made a poster for the HCDE capstone showcase and pitched the design to a panel of judges. I also produced and filmed a video prototype for our sponsor and the judges.

The closest thing to a real product cycle I had in school: a sponsor, a deadline, and testing that changed our minds more than once.
