Cooking Central

A lot of young people see cooking as a chore. They don't have much time, money, or skill, and during the pandemic it got lonely too. Our team designed an app to get people cooking more, and cooking with others.

ContextUW Intro to User-Centered Design
When2020
TeamTeam project
ToolsFigma, Miro
At a glance2 min read
Problem

Young adults see cooking as a chore, and a lonely one.

What I did

Interviews, personas, a journey map, 8 requirements, Figma prototypes, and testing.

Result

An app with meal planning, search by ingredients, and a social feed.

ResearchDesign requirementsDesignTesting and iteration

Research

We interviewed college students and young adults about cooking. They talked about not knowing what to make, not having the right ingredients, and feeling isolated in the kitchen. We grouped our notes with affinity mapping.

We made two personas: someone who's never really cooked, and a beginner who wants to get better.

Our two personas

Then we mapped the whole cooking journey. The low points were deciding what to make with no time, and improvising when an ingredient was missing. The high points were finding an exciting recipe and sharing the result with friends and family.

Design requirements

  1. Show how long each recipe takes.
  2. Find recipes that fit the ingredients you have.
  3. Suggest recipes based on preferences and skill level.
  4. Keep people engaged while cooking, with video or chat.
  5. Give clear steps with visuals.
  6. Explain cooking terms people don't know.
  7. Let people get help from other users.
  8. Let people share photos and recipes.
Two storyboards for different scenarios

Design

We mapped the app's structure and color-coded the main flows to cut out unnecessary screens. Then we built low-fidelity prototypes in Figma so we could test and change things fast.

Testing and iteration

  • Calendar: people liked it but didn't want to set exact meal times. We moved to date-based planning, a weekly view, and a shopping list built from your recipes.
  • Search: two search icons on the home page confused people, and they wanted filters for meal type, appliances, and more diets.
  • Social: one person found it messy, another preferred browsing over posting. We redesigned it to support both, borrowing ideas from Reddit.

We did this fully remote during the pandemic, which taught me how to keep a team moving when nobody is in the same room.
