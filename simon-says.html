Simon Says

An electronic Simon Says. The game flashes a sequence of lights, you repeat it on arcade buttons, and it gets longer every round. I had one week to design, build, and present it.

ContextUW HCDE
WhenOne week
BuiltSolo
HardwareArduino, arcade buttons, LEDs
At a glance2 min read
Problem

Design, build, and present a hardware game in one week.

What I did

Circuit design, Arduino code, a cardboard enclosure, and sound cues for accessibility.

Result

A working game and demo video, on schedule.

The planDesign and buildDemo

The plan

One week for concept, design, build, and presentation meant I had to plan tightly:

  • Day 1: code that generates and tracks a random button order.
  • Day 3: the circuit, with a start button and arcade buttons.
  • Day 4: a working prototype, with a buzzer and score screen as stretch goals.
  • Day 6: final presentation and documentation.

I had to rewire arcade buttons from Amazon, which meant learning the components and pin layouts fast.

Design and build

I diagrammed the circuit and paired each button with its matching LED, so the light cues lined up with gameplay. Everything went in a cardboard box with holes cut for the buttons. The Arduino and breadboard sat hidden inside, so players only saw the game.

The code is split into functions for generating the sequence, flashing the LEDs, playing sounds, checking input, and moving to the next round. Each button also has its own buzz, so the game works for people who can't see the lights.

Demo

The hardest part was keeping the sequence, button presses, and LED feedback in sync. The tight timeline also taught me to stop polishing and ship.
