Save For Later

Lots of Gopuff shoppers left items in their cart just to remember them, then never checked out. I looked at whether a Save for Later feature would fit how people actually shop.

WhenSummer 2022

Problem

Shoppers used the cart as a wishlist, then abandoned it.

What I did

The ask

Gopuff wanted to know if a Save for Later feature would fit people's shopping habits, and what would get them to create, organize, and share lists.

Approach

I combined interviews and usability tests in one 30-minute session, so I got the why and the how from every participant.

Six people talked through how they shop, why they keep lists in other apps, and what alerts they'd want on saved items.

Then they used a prototype to save items and manage them in a Saved for Later section.

The prototype

I worked with the UX designers to get mockups ready for testing.

Other prototype screens

What we learned

  • People were already using the cart as a list. They parked items to compare them, to buy later, or just to remember them.
  • Price drives it. Alerts about sales or low stock were the thing most likely to turn a saved item into a purchase.
  • "Save for Later," "Favorites," and "Wishlists" meant different things to people depending on when they planned to buy.
  • People wanted multiple named lists, clear labels, and quick access to product details.
  • Sharing mattered for gift ideas, family shopping, and party planning.
  • One-tap actions were a big deal, like adding to a list or moving something to the cart.
  • The flag icon confused people. Most didn't know what it did.

Recommendations

  1. Show full item details in lists: image, name, specs, price, and price changes.
  2. Let people make multiple named lists.
  3. Keep lists until the user deletes them.
  4. Promote deals on popular items people have saved.
  5. Add sharing and collaboration.
  6. Recommend more items based on what's already in a list.
  7. Replace the flag icon with text or a clearer icon.
  8. Notify people when saved items go on sale or are about to sell out.

What I learned

Timely alerts create urgency, and urgency moves people. One unclear icon can sink a feature. And convenience kept showing up as a top three priority.
