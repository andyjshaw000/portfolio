Gopuff · Research

Bundling

Gopuff wanted more people buying bundles, and bigger orders when they did. I ran the research that shaped how bundles look and work in the app.

The ask

Gopuff is an on-demand delivery app. The product team was about to run experiments on bundle color, layout, targeting, and placement, and wanted to know what users actually wanted before spending engineering time.

My job: find out which bundles people liked and what would raise adoption and average order value.

Planning and recruiting

I wrote the research brief with my mentor, Rebecca Rago, and split the study into two phases: interviews for the why, then concept tests on prototypes for what actually worked.

I wrote the screener and recruited Gopuff and competitor users through userinterviews.com.

The screener, participant summary, and recruiting platform

Interviews and concept tests

I interviewed six participants about how they shop, how they use discounts, how bundles should be shown, what should go in them, whether they wanted to customize them, and what kind of discount they'd expect.

Six more people used early interactive prototypes. They found a bundle, added it to their bag, and edited it. Then they compared three designs side by side on looks, ease of use, and where things sat on the screen.

Working with design

I set the timeline with the UX designers so mockups were ready before each round. People reacted to real screens instead of descriptions.

What we learned

  • A lot of people never noticed bundles. Several said they'd look for them inside categories like Snacks or Grocery, not on the homepage.
  • People wanted to customize bundles for their own tastes or for an occasion.
  • The discount had to be obvious. Crossed-out prices and clear labels mattered more than almost anything else.
  • 3 to 6 items felt manageable. Under 5 was the sweet spot unless the discount was big.
  • "Add all to bag" tested well, and color cues on bundled items helped people keep track.
  • People asked for occasion bundles for parties, holidays, and meal prep.

Recommendations and impact

I wrote a stakeholder report covering discounts, bundle size, use cases, and placement. These were the recommendations:

  1. Tell users when they're a few items away from completing a bundle.
  2. Keep bundles at five items or fewer.
  3. Make "Add all" bigger and easier to spot.
  4. Rewrite the carousel header to call out the savings.
  5. Show the bundle subtotal as its own line in the cart.
  6. Mark bundled items in the bag with a color cue on the left.

Most of these were in the live app by August 2023.

What I'd do differently

Keep recruiting titles neutral so participants aren't tipped off, and plan for no-shows from day one.

And leave the guide when someone says something interesting. Some of my best findings came from those detours.
